# v3 validation: action conditioning and discard ties

Source SHA-256: `2a831f886d03232967bc826ee25a6ad013797c9810308e970ace229601f650cd`.

This version weights sampled opponent hands using public call/raise sizes and historical boards. It retains the latest street context and strongest earlier aggression, rather than letting a later small call erase that evidence. Weak hands retain positive likelihood. It also resolves exact Monte Carlo selection-equity ties by current made-hand strength, preserving a full house or quads without overriding a strictly higher-equity draw. The sample budget is 24 worlds against up to three opponents and 16 against larger fields.

## Final-source validation

- Remote real-engine fixture validation: **342 states**, covering 2, 3, 8 and 10 players, with **90 malformed/truncated-memory cases**; all passed.
- Remote timing over **3,420 decisions**: p99 **0.625 ms**, maximum **0.690 ms** for the tested states. These are observed measurements, not a guarantee for all workloads or hardware.
- Remote zero-bank probe: **255 candidate decisions, zero failures**, with the normal 1 ms decision allowance and zero time bank.
- Required local-simulator integration on the build box: **200 three-player tables** against the default check-or-fold and minimum-raise bots, with zero failed decisions.
- Focused review: **13 checks passed**, including causal exposure handling, increasing value-to-air likelihood ratios as bets/raises grow, a positive weak-hand likelihood, and preservation of an earlier street's board. Private fixture data are excluded from this repository; the public range regressions reproduce the generic model properties.
- Portable discard regression suite: **126 synthetic cases** cover card-order permutations, 1/2/3/7/9 opponents, made full houses/quads/straight flushes, and preserving a uniquely higher-equity draw. The original v1 fails 15 of these cases; v3 passes all 126. The original source is retained in Git history for comparison.

These checks establish legality and the tested corrections. They do not establish tournament-field strength. The 800-table report under `benchmarks/` is historical v1 evidence, not a v3 benchmark. The model remains a chip-EV heuristic with a small importance-sampling budget, no learned opponent parameters, no fold-equity model, and no ICM optimization.
